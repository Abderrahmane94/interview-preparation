## How do you define a good developer?
**Why ask this**: The answer reveals the company's values and what they actually reward. A good culture values craftsmanship, collaboration, and learning — not just ticket velocity.

**What to listen for**:
- Do they mention soft skills (communication, ownership, teamwork) alongside technical ones?
- Do they describe someone who ships *and* maintains quality? Or purely someone who goes fast?
- Is the bar about individual performance or team impact?

**Red flags**: "Someone who never blocks others" (could mean pressure to never raise issues), or "someone who does what they're told" (could signal lack of autonomy).

**What a strong answer looks like**: A good developer solves the right problem (not just the assigned ticket), communicates early when something is unclear, writes code their teammates can maintain, and continuously learns.

## Can you tell me about the company culture?
**Why ask this**: Culture directly affects your day-to-day experience, growth, and wellbeing. Job descriptions don't show the real culture — the way people answer this question does.

**What to listen for**:
- **Feedback culture**: Do they give regular 1-on-1s? Is feedback constructive and two-way?
- **Work-life balance**: What does a typical day look like? Are evenings and weekends respected?
- **Learning and growth**: Do they invest in training, conferences, internal talks, time for R&D?
- **Collaboration style**: Cross-functional teams, pair programming, code reviews — or solo silos?
- **Transparency**: Are decisions explained top-down, or does the team have input?

**Red flags**: Vague answers like "we work hard and play hard", or dismissing the question quickly. Also watch for: "we're like a family" (can mean blurred boundaries), or "everyone wears many hats" (can mean understaffed).

**Follow-up questions you can ask**:
- "Can you describe a typical week for someone in this role?"
- "How does the team handle disagreements about technical decisions?"
- "What does career progression look like here?"
